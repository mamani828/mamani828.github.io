About

I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering in the joint doctoral program between San Diego State University and UC San Diego. I am very fortunate to be advised by Prof. Reza Akhavian (DiCE Lab, SDSU) and Prof. Nikolay Atanasov (Existential Robotics Lab, UCSD).

My research focuses on robot planning under constraints that extend beyond collision checking, especially high-dimensional and human-facing requirements. I am an ARCS Foundation Scholar and a recipient of the SDSU University Graduate Fellowship. I received my B.S. in Cognitive Science, specializing in Machine Learning and Neural Computation, from UC San Diego.

Research Interests

I study how robots can plan safe, useful actions when geometric collision checks alone are insufficient. My work develops ways to represent and satisfy higher-dimensional constraints involving robot state, task context, and people, including ergonomic risk in human-robot interaction. I use semantic scene representations, control barrier functions, language-guided planning, latent world models, and learning-based methods to bring these constraints into robot decision-making.
